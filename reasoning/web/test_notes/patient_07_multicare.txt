A 53-year-old male with multiple comorbidities, including acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS) (cluster of differentiation 4 (CD4) 14 cells/microL, viral load 109,294 copies/mL), presented with constant, generalized abdominal pain for five days, accompanied by subjective fever. He reported "black" vomit that became blood-streaked. On arrival at the ED, the patient was in acute distress. Vitals were significant for a pulse rate of 135/min, respiratory rate of 36/min, and blood pressure of 55/45 mmHg. He appeared cachectic and had a distended abdomen with diffuse tenderness and guarding, typical of an acute abdomen. Labs were notable for elevated lactate levels of 8.3 mmol/L and high anion gap (anion gap 21mEq/L) metabolic acidosis, suggesting sepsis.
